The remaining decisions do not warrant findings at the requested confidence threshold:

- **Decision 1 — keep readiness and posting separate.** This split is justified by different domain questions, independently of Admin API stability. Purchasability, impersonation and preview restrictions can prevent expansion without stopping an existing policy. Combining the predicates would obscure that distinction. Two copy tables are also reasonable because they explain different consequences. The more consequential duplication is the settings projection’s relationship to the worker’s authority, addressed above.
- **Decision 3 — retain the explicit order and `tierChangeDirection`.** This is a small abstraction with several present consumers, and it replaces two duplicate arrays. A named `isDowngradeTo` would not eliminate the ordering needed by the cards and price presentation. Three tiers remain straightforward **if they form a genuine autonomy ladder**.
- **Bundling is defensible here.** The shared staging and rollback unit creates coupling, but the changes are bounded, disjoint and introduce no migration or irreversible state transition. I would prefer separate commits and independently recorded staging outcomes for each tracer. I would not require three branches solely to eliminate this manageable coupling.
- **The glossary is close to its cap, but this addition improves it.** The new distinction belongs in the glossary, and the existing shape check forces a future split before the cap is exceeded. At 2,964 words, proximity to the limit alone is insufficient reason to expand this branch into a documentation restructuring.

**Low-confidence appendix — not grounds for revision**

- A third tier may offer more locations or different limits without changing whether automatic posting is permitted. In that case, commercial plan order, autonomy order and **loss of automatic-posting entitlement** become separate concepts. The current modal ties its total-autonomy-loss warning to every downward move. When a third tier is designed, derive that warning from the actual entitlement transition, even if an ordered list still determines Upgrade/Downgrade labels. No current third-tier contract establishes this as a present defect.
