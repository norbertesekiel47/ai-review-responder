# RepWorth brand story & messaging

Owner of the **narrative** layer of the brand: positioning, story arc, and
messaging guardrails for anything customer-facing (landing pages, ads, video,
social, decks). Visual identity is owned elsewhere — tokens by
`src/app/globals.css`, layout/components by [`brand-island.md`](./brand-island.md).

## Positioning

**RepWorth is the first true AI agent for reputation — not a reply tool.**

- The commodity claim (avoid leading with it): "AI writes your review replies."
  Dozens of tools say this.
- The differentiated claim (lead with it): **trusted autonomy** — a tireless
  agent tends your reputation while you run your business, and *nothing goes
  public without passing a gate*. The gated pipeline (draft → moderate → judge
  → approve/veto) is the part no competitor's ad can honestly copy.
- Long-term vision: the best agentic online reputation platform — more
  platforms beyond Google, more capabilities (soliciting reviews, flagging and
  disputing false reviews, monitoring everywhere). Autonomy ladder:
  Copilot → Guardian → Autopilot.

**Tagline of record:** `The AI agent for your reputation`

## The story arc (canonical)

Every piece of brand storytelling follows the same transformation:

1. **Chaos** — reviews pile up unanswered while the owner works.
2. **The agent steps in** — each review answered, in the owner's voice.
3. **Care and control** — the gate moment: checked, approved, released.
   This is the signature beat; give it stillness.
4. **Public repair/reply** — answered publicly; replies are a public act.
5. **Compounding** — reputation assembles into an owned asset (stars,
   constellation, rising reply rate).
6. **Widening (final ~20% only)** — pull back: this was one node; other
   platforms and capabilities are *warming up* around the same agent.
7. **Logo lock** — the agent motif resolves into the brand mark.

The emotional register is **relief and trust**, not speed or tech.

## Story-emphasis one-liner (paste into any creative brief)

> A tireless agent answering every review while the owner runs their business —
> with visible moments of care and control before anything goes public.
> Emphasize the transformation: unanswered reviews piling up → each answered,
> checked, and released → reputation compounding. Then, in the final seconds,
> widen the frame: the same agent reaching further — new platforms lighting up,
> new capabilities activating (requesting reviews, flagging false ones,
> monitoring everywhere). End on the sense that this is the first full AI agent
> for reputation — and it's just getting started.

Alternate emphases by audience:

- **Cold audience (outcome-weighted):** the owner gets their evenings back;
  reviews answer themselves at 3 AM; rating and reply rate climb while the
  shop's lights are off.
- **Investors/partners (vision-weighted):** the first true reputation *agent*
  — perceives every review, decides with judgment gates, acts only with earned
  autonomy; Copilot → Guardian → Autopilot as an ascending sequence.

## Messaging guardrails

- **Roadmap honesty.** Unshipped capabilities (review solicitation, false-review
  disputes, non-Google platforms) may be *visually* gestured at — dim nodes
  "warming up" — but never named in copy as present-tense shipped claims.
  Closing lines stay aspirational-but-true (e.g. "Reputation, handled.").
- **Expansion is verbs, not icons.** If roadmap capabilities appear as text,
  use two-word verb phrases ("Get reviews · Flag fakes · Watch everywhere") —
  verbs read as agency; icon grids read as a pricing page.
- **One thing per ad.** Prove the core story deeply, then widen at the end.
  An ad that says "we do everything" says nothing.
- **Anti-slop copy rules** (mirrors brand-island): no "unlock", "supercharge",
  "seamless", no fake metrics/testimonials/logos.
- **SMB texture is specific, not abstract.** The customer is a café at 8 AM,
  a salon, a dental office — show their craft, not a generic "business owner".
