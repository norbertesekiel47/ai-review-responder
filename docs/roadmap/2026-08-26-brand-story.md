# RepWorth brand story (source of truth for marketing narrative)

This file is the single source for the RepWorth story across marketing
campaigns, social media, videos, landing pages, and the website. Copy teams
and future sessions pull from here; they do not invent a parallel narrative.
It is consistent with `2026-08-26-positioning-governed-autonomy.md` (the
claims discipline lives there) and with the current-state doc (what is true
today vs vision).

Rules for using this file:

1. Every public claim must come from the proof-point table in the positioning
   doc §2, or be clearly framed as vision (see §8 below, the honesty ledger).
2. Never fabricate reviews, testimonials, or customer results in creative.
   Demo data is labeled demo. (FTC Consumer Reviews Rule discipline is part
   of the brand, not just the product.)
3. UI/page work that carries this story still routes through the
   `repworth-brand-design` skill and the existing-routes rule.

## 3. The full narrative (the master story — landing pages, videos, decks)

**Act 1 — The problem.** For a local business, reputation *is* the
storefront. Customers read reviews before they call — and now AI assistants
read them too, and recommend accordingly. Every unanswered review, every
slow reply, every unhappy customer who never heard back quietly costs jobs,
covers, and appointments. But the people who own these businesses are
operators — they're fixing the furnace, plating the dish, finishing the
appointment. Nobody opened a plumbing company to spend evenings on Google.

**Act 2 — The turn.** AI can write review replies now. Everyone's AI can —
that's the problem. Your public reputation is your name, in public, forever.
Handing it to an unsupervised text generator is how a bad week becomes a
screenshot. The market is full of "AI agents" for reputation. Almost nobody
answers the owner's real question: *who's watching the agent?*

**Act 3 — RepWorth's answer.** RepWorth was built governance-first, autonomy
second. You set the rules once — which reviews it may answer on its own,
which always come to you, how many per day, what it may never say. Then it
works: it learns your voice from your own edits, drafts every reply, asks
every eligible customer for a review (and visibly refuses the asks it
shouldn't send), and escalates the complaints that need a human. Above it
sit two watchdogs no one else ships: an independent AI **Judge** that
reviews 100% of automatic posts — if the Judge is down, automatic posting
stops — and a **Governor** that suspends autonomy on its own if quality
slips. Every action lands on a permanent audit trail, and you hold a veto
window before anything automatic goes live. Start on **Copilot**, where you
approve everything and RepWorth learns your standards. Graduate to
**Autopilot**, where it runs inside them.

**Act 4 — The vision** *(always framed as where this is going)*. The day
this is finished, a business owner wakes up to one message: *"RepWorth
handled 14 new reviews overnight, sent 38 review requests from yesterday's
completed jobs, answered 11 routine reviews automatically, escalated two
complaints, noticed that arrival-time complaints are your fastest-growing
issue, and recovered one unhappy customer. Nothing else needs your
attention."* Reputation stops being a chore and becomes a system — one that
fixes the experience, not just the stars.

## 4. Brand pillars (the five values — public wording)

These reinforce the category; use them verbatim in About pages, decks, and
culture content.

1. **Earn trust. Never manufacture it.** Real reputations are earned. We
   make real customer experiences visible — we never fake, filter, or buy
   sentiment. *(Product: no review gating, no incentives, no fabricated
   engagement.)*
2. **Humans set the rules. AI does the work.** Automation should give owners
   more control, not less. You define the voice, policies, and boundaries;
   RepWorth handles the repetition. *(Product: Copilot/Autopilot, trust
   dial, veto window.)*
3. **Fix the experience, not just the stars.** A bad review is a signal, not
   a score. We help you find what went wrong, make it right, and get
   better. *(Product: escalation today; recovery workflows and topic
   intelligence on the roadmap.)*
4. **Customer data is borrowed, not owned.** Businesses and their customers
   trust us with sensitive information. We collect what the job needs,
   protect it, and use it for nothing else. *(Product: consent and
   suppression ledgers, tenant isolation, audit logs, offboarding with real
   deletion.)*
5. **Be measurably useful.** AI that demos well but changes nothing is
   noise. We measure ourselves in time saved, reviews answered, customers
   recovered, and problems surfaced. *(Product: edit-distance tracking,
   timeSaved, outcome instrumentation; conservative attribution only.)*

Together they read as one philosophy: **Earn trust → Give humans control →
Fix reality → Protect data → Prove value.** Pillar 1 is the centerpiece.

## 5. Voice and tone

- **Operator-to-operator.** Plain words, short sentences, zero martech
  jargon. Say "review requests," not "reputation acquisition funnels."
- **Calm confidence, not hype.** The governance story lets us underclaim:
  we're the vendor telling you *what our AI is not allowed to do*. That
  restraint IS the brand.
- **Specific beats superlative.** "An AI Judge checks 100% of automatic
  posts" outperforms "enterprise-grade AI safety." Never "revolutionary,"
  "seamless," "supercharge."
- **Honest about autonomy.** We say "runs inside your rules," never "set it
  and forget it."
- **"AI employee" is category language, not a differentiator.** Competitors
  already print it (Vendasta's MARiO, RingCentral's AI Receptionist). Say
  it to name the category; prove it with governance (the Judge, the veto
  window, the audit trail) and measured hours saved.

## 6. Channel playbook

### Landing page (hero → arc)

Hero: one-liner (§1) + tagline. Section order mirrors the narrative: the
problem (Act 1) → who's watching the agent (Act 2) → how governance works
(Act 3, with the proof-point chips: Judge · veto window · Governor · audit
trail · your rules) → Copilot/Autopilot ladder → vision (Act 4, labeled).
CTA: "Put your reputation on governed autopilot" / secondary "See how the
Judge works."

### Social media (recurring post formats)

1. **"The agent said no."** Restraint stories: show a (demo-labeled)
   withhold decision and its reason. Nobody else can post these; they are
   the moat made visible.
2. **Reply makeovers.** Demo review → generic AI reply vs RepWorth reply in
   a learned voice, with the "why RepWorth wrote this" framing (label demo).
3. **Governance explainers.** 30-second "what is the veto window / who is
   the Judge / what does the Governor do" series.
4. **Operator empathy.** The 9pm-on-Google owner moment; problem-first
   posts with no product until the last line.
5. **Vertical vocabulary posts** (post-Phase 3): "your rating says 4.6; the
   real story is arrival-time sentiment" — using ontology terms.

### Video scripts (skeletons)

**30s:** Owner closing up at night, phone buzzes — a harsh review. Cut:
"Everyone will sell you an AI that replies. Ask them who watches it." Beat
the governance stack in four fast frames (rules → Judge → veto → record).
Close on tagline + logo.

**90s:** Follow one review end-to-end through the system — arrives, triaged,
drafted in the owner's voice, held (it's a 1-star complaint, policy says
human), owner taps approve from their phone, posted, Judge verdict lands,
audit-trail entry shown. Narration is Act 2 + Act 3 condensed. End with Act
4 vision, spoken as future ("The day this is finished…").

### Ads / short hooks

- "Who's watching your AI?"
- "Your reputation runs itself. By your rules."
- "The reputation agent with a governor on it."
- "Never write a review reply from scratch again." *(Copilot, true today)*
- "Every automatic post, checked by a Judge. Every action, on the record."

## 7. Audience variants

- **Owner (default):** the story above, feature-forward, time-back framed.
- **Investor:** open with the structural transition — every local business
  will delegate work to AI agents; reputation is the natural first
  delegation because it is thousands of small repetitive judgments; but no
  one hands an ungoverned AI their public identity. RepWorth is the
  *governed* reputation agent: business memory, workflow memory, a
  reputation graph, and trust infrastructure that makes autonomy sellable.
  Headline metric once usage accumulates: autonomy rate rising while
  correction rate falls (the run spine already records both inputs).
- **Agency/white-label (later, Phase 8):** "governed autonomy you can
  resell without babysitting it" — do not market this before the offering
  exists.

## 8. Honesty ledger — which story elements may be used WHEN

Extends the positioning doc §5 gate. Three buckets; check before publishing.

| **Built, awaiting prod flip** | Autopilot auto-posting, Judge verdicts on production posts, solicitation real sends | Only as "how it works" / staging-proven — present-tense customer claims unlock at the §8.4 prod flag flips (tie copy release to the flag-flip checklist) |
| **Vision (roadmap Phases 1–7)** | Job-completion triggered requests, QR-in-store *(pre-launch item — moves to "true today" when it ships)*, recovery workflows, topic intelligence ("arrival-time sentiment fell"), Business Truth explanations, autonomy matrix, Impact page, multi-platform | Always future-framed ("where this is going"), never implied as shipped |

The Act 4 morning-report scene is permanently vision-framed until recovery
(Phase 2) and topic intelligence (Phase 3) actually ship; after that, rebuild
it from a real (consented, anonymized) account.
