# CEO/CTO — 90-day task brief

Owner: the founder acting as CEO and CTO. Draft assignment period: **September 8–December 6, 2026**. Read the [shared plan](../README.md) for company targets, cash gates, approval rules and measurement definitions. Everything below starts as planned.

Strategy revised **September 11** with the owner's approval; task completion
still requires dated evidence. The shared calendar and cash limits remain.

**Your job:** decide which customer problem is worth winning, make the product reliably handle it, and lead the decisions and investor conversations that keep the company able to execute.

The COO owns the pitch document and customer operations; the CFO owns the model and cash; the CMO owns acquisition and messaging. You supply decisions, product truth and the demonstration. Their work should free you from being the default owner of every task.

## Weekly capacity

Plan for 40 hours: 12 product investigation/delivery, 8 customer discovery and key sales calls with COO, 6 funding preparation/meetings with COO, 4 company decisions and founder handoffs, 4 architecture/quality/capacity work tied to the selected workflow, 6 buffer/support. Meetings count inside these allocations. Reallocate after cash or customer evidence changes; the extra 20 hours is optional peak capacity, not a second product workstream.

The existing **20 engineering hours total for the first selected improvement** (with the owner-operator v1 as the one named 60-hour exception, 30 committed) remains a provisional ceiling, including discovery, implementation and verification. The weekly capacity above is not permission to start new speculative feature work. Confirm the first scope against your actual workload. Discovery for a connector uses at most four of those 20 hours. Failed discovery closes the candidate; a larger scope needs an explicit revision, not quiet overtime.

Treat the 12+4 product hours as **available capacity**, not 16 promised engineering hours every week. Track every hour attributable to the first improvement against its 20-hour total. Unused capacity goes to approved customer conversations, funding decisions or existing customer support. Later improvement budgets need a new explicit selection and cash/capacity decision. No quarter-long allocation overrides the first-scope cap.

## First 48 hours

1. Confirm the four-founder ownership table, decision rights and working hours. Ask COO to own a single task/decision register.
2. Give CFO the actual product/service commitments and your minimum support needs through the private finance workspace. Decide the continuation date using cash reality.
3. Join COO/CMO in observing an accessible owner's existing workflow. Watch what they do, how long it takes and what they would otherwise ignore.
4. Confirm the available demo environment and current product journey. Supply a brief/demo to COO within **two combined CEO+COO hours** using existing material; recording a mocked action is not evidence of real publication.
5. Review the lawyer-screening brief prepared by COO and approve a specific next action. Record the exact personal filing date separately with appropriate professional advice.
6. Decide the paid offer and bounded first outreach batch proposed by COO/CMO. A request for approval should already include recipients/segment, message, channel and any cost.

Decide eligible roster contact separately from counsel engagement, ideally September 8 evening; a missing counsel quote alone does not block a permitted conversation. Review CFO/CMO's optional prepayment case September 9, including the service obligation it creates, before any offer or billing change. Give CFO your own support requirement, binding date and personal agreement to work under each cash scenario; do not assume the other founders agree to reduced or unpaid work.

Evidence sought: one dated decision sheet, actual customer observation notes, current demo limitations, approved or explicitly pending outreach/legal actions. These are not assumed done because this brief exists.

These are contributions to the shared plan's **five first-week records**, not separate documents to finish before customer contact. Prioritize approved paid-offer attempts to the existing suitable roster by September 10; initial reconciliation/first attempts are limited to two working days, while research for the next approved batch can continue. COO owns the canonical roster, customer-value/renewal notes and decisions; CFO owns cash and funnel/cost numbers. Your product notes add technical evidence and reference those records.

## What you measure

- Correctly completed eligible work, failures and owner corrections, with raw counts and dates.
- Net recurring owner effort for work previously done; setup effort and founder support reported separately. The fixed seven-minute dashboard estimate is not observed savings.
- Time from a selected burden/blocker to a verified fix or an experiment decision; rejected, inconclusive or paused candidates stay visible.
- Paying/continuing users of the selected workflow with COO/CFO. Low review volume is an evidence limit, not automatically a poor product or a churn event.
- Current intake capacity, support interruptions and the next verified technical constraint.
- Which investor or buyer objections actually changed a decision, rather than the number of slides or features completed.

Draft outcome targets are in the shared plan. You do not own a separate customer-count forecast. Count one company outcome once even when several founders contributed.

## Decision and delegation rules

Make routine scope decisions within one working day of receiving enough information; state what is missing if you cannot. COO coordinates agendas and follow-through so decisions do not remain in chat. Reserve your direct involvement for product scope, quality risks, key customer/investor conversations and company cash choices.

The operator can reproduce issues, inspect code, draft specs, implement authorized fixes, run appropriate checks, prepare sanitized demos and challenge the plan. Sub-agents can own bounded tasks with a clear file/output boundary. You remain accountable for product and company decisions; an agent report is not customer acceptance.

Keep API/MCP work conditional on an actual prospective user and recurring task. Keep architecture investment proportional to the next credible workload. Ask COO/CMO for buyer evidence when you feel pulled toward broad UI polish or “more agentic” features without a specific problem.

## Evidence and weekly report

Save sanitized work in this folder. Proposed filenames above can be sections in an existing record, created when a real task requires them; they are not mandatory separate files or completed artifacts. Put private identities, customer messages or financing records in the approved private workspace and reference them with appropriate access. The CEO/CTO role is one workstream; individual task IDs do not each trigger another two-round debate. Major decisions and actual workstream completion retain both rounds, combined with other roles when the checkpoint coincides.

Use one five-line update each Friday:

```text
Done: [task ID, actual accepted result; otherwise say preparation completed]
Blocked: [dependency, owner and needed decision]
Next: [one principal outcome and due date]
Evidence: [openable link, measurement dates, source/environment where relevant]
Risk: [what could prevent the next outcome and the current response]
```

Day-90 completion means the selected work and results have evidence, open limitations are named, and the next company decision is recorded. It does not require every possible task or roadmap topic to be implemented.
