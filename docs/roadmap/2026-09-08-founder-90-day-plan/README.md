## What blitzscaling means here

Hoffman and Yeh describe blitzscaling as favoring speed over efficiency under uncertainty. Their framework also considers distribution, market size, margins, product-market fit and the ability to scale operations. It does not establish that RepWorth already has these conditions. Sources: [the authors' book site](https://www.blitzscaling.com/) and [Chris Yeh's explanation of growth factors and limits](https://www.blitzscalingvc.com/post/the-blitzscalability-scorecard-identifying-future-market-winners).

Our application is a **blitzscaling preparation and conditional acceleration plan**. RepWorth's market leadership opportunity, strong network effects and repeatable acquisition are unproven. It would be misleading to label ordinary early customer discovery as full blitzscaling.

The speed choices we can make now are concrete:

- Four founders work in parallel on product, delivery, money and distribution, with one owner for each decision.
- Sell the useful current workflow while learning; a polished website or every roadmap feature is not a dependency for a customer conversation.
- Use manual setup, a simple evidence sheet and direct founder help to learn quickly. Show those hours in delivery cost and plan to remove repeated work. This approach is also consistent with [Paul Graham's early-user recruiting advice](https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html).
- Keep one active engineering improvement and one primary acquisition experiment. Batch decisions and reuse real demos and customer evidence across sales, product and the pitch.
- Once repeat use, acquisition and affordable delivery are demonstrated, concentrate an approved larger budget and founder time on the winning channel. Accept temporary inefficiency deliberately, with an amount at risk, an owner and a review date.
- Test what could make an advantage accumulate: more accurate owner-approved business context, fewer corrections, useful integrations, and distribution through trusted partners. A database of reviews or an MCP endpoint alone does not establish a moat.

## Cash comes before the later calendar

On **September 8**, CFO defines the **minimum operation without new funding**: obligations required to honor existing beta/customer commitments, unavoidable company bills, and the minimum founder support needed for people to carry the work. Show amounts, dates, available cleared cash, who does the remaining work and the first uncovered obligation. Separate unavoidable obligations from proposed reductions that still need approval. An unpaid founder role cannot be assumed available indefinitely.

Each founder supplies CFO privately on September 8: minimum support needed, their own binding date, and whether they personally agree to continue at the proposed support level, for how many hours and until when. Show actual availability under each scenario in the cash/decision record. The CEO cannot consent to another founder's unpaid work. Personal details stay private; company-facing records use only approved operational totals and availability. Any reduction/pause decision is a material strategy checkpoint and receives both required Claude rounds.

Compare that floor with the proposed fuller plan. If even the minimum operation cannot be covered, bring the decision to the CEO and founding team that day. Do not assume the company can coast until October 7. The operative decision is the earliest cash/support deadline minus the agreed lead-time buffer, including any earlier date to avoid a new commitment. If inputs are missing, record the unresolved decision immediately and use September 21 only as the existing provisional meeting date, never as evidence the company can wait.

The CEO records **funded fuller scope / affordable minimum scope / further reduction or pause requiring a decision**. Later weeks describe conditional choices, activated only when cleared cash covers their authorized cost and intervening obligations through the next decision. A loan, investment or future subscription is not counted as received. The calendar does not assume any of them will arrive. Financing discovery begins now; it does not wait for customer proof or a new feature.

**September 9 offer decision:** CFO and CMO add one optional way to receive customer cash earlier alongside the existing monthly offer. CFO checks total contribution, service obligations, refunds, payment fees and timing; use full price or reject the variant if it is unsuitable. ## When to accelerate

Use this gate at Days 30/60/90 and before expanding beyond the explicitly approved initial learning allowance or committing to hiring. Initial customer discovery needs a bounded sample before any payer exists: CMO/COO propose its total contact limit, test period and delivery capacity; CFO shows cost; CEO approves the exact contact/spend scope. Moving through approved subsets of that sample is not a new scaling commitment. No approval is supplied by this draft. The following are provisional operating thresholds, not statistical proof of product-market fit.

1. **Buyer evidence:** seek at least three independent paying accounts in the chosen group, two openable customer-value records, and actual continuation/renewal decisions where due. Report the sample and disconfirming cases. A three-account success permits a small next experiment, not a large market claim.
2. **Acquisition evidence:** the same offer/channel has produced qualified opportunities and paid decisions across two separately measured batches. Use raw counts with conversion rates; one enthusiastic contact is not a repeatable channel.
3. **Delivery evidence:** COO and CEO demonstrate the proposed next intake can fit onboarding/support capacity. Track eligible work correctly completed, missed work, corrections and founder minutes. Any unresolved defect that could cause wrong-account access or unauthorized customer-facing actions blocks expanding the affected workflow.
4. **Financial evidence:** CFO shows the maximum experiment loss, dated obligations and ability to fund the next decision period without counting uncommitted investment as received. Early fully loaded losses may be acceptable if explicitly approved and bounded; positive company-wide profit is not a prerequisite.
5. **Reason speed matters:** CEO and CMO document the opportunity being captured, such as a real partner opening or a repeatedly converting segment, and why delay would cost opportunity. They explain what advantage might compound and what could disprove it.

If these hold, propose a **two-week expansion of one working channel**, normally increasing the preceding batch by no more than 2× within the approved cash and support cap. This is a starting rule, adjustable by a recorded decision. If they fail, keep authorized customer service running, correct the specific blocker and retest. Funding activity and useful customer conversations can continue. Read-only MCP, new platforms, extra industries and hiring each need their own case; they are not automatic rewards for reaching a date.

Use one customer record from first contact through onboarding, value review and renewal; one person owns its next action. Keep investor records separate. Agree a single reporting definition in the first week instead of running four spreadsheets with conflicting totals. Prospecting activity owned by a founder is distinct from RepWorth sending review requests on a customer's behalf.

**Approval:** this package authorizes drafting, not contacting people or spending. CEO approval must identify recipients or a bounded recipient segment, channel, purpose, message/template, cadence and any budget. Record scope once and reuse it within its bounds; seek a new decision when scope changes. Spending, publishing customer proof, hiring, funding terms, legal engagement and account changes follow actual authority and the owner's instructions. Avoid sending private financial/customer data to the repository or a reviewer.

Keep **separately dated approval lines** in the one packet: roster contact under verified current terms, ready for a September 8 evening decision; counsel engagement/spend; and any new prepayment offer. An incomplete counsel comparison does not hold up an otherwise eligible contact decision. Approval to hold a conversation does not authorize a product launch, altered terms, legal engagement or expenditure. Surface any actual legal restriction affecting a specific action rather than assuming all actions share one dependency.

## Weekly rhythm and evidence

- Monday: 30-minute founder decision meeting. Each founder brings up to three priorities, one blocker and a specific decision request. COO updates the shared plan.
- Wednesday: brief written numbers and blockers; meet only when a decision cannot wait.
- Friday: 30-minute results review, including cash, customers, quality and the next acquisition experiment. CFO supplies numbers, COO keeps one decision log.
- Each founder saves **five lines** weekly: **done / blocked / next / evidence / risk**. Include links a teammate can open and date the measurement. Done means an accepted deliverable exists, or an operational result has its own evidence.
- At each workstream completion or major strategic checkpoint, run the requested **two Claude challenge rounds**. Challenge the complete proposed next plan, address three strongest objections, revise, challenge again, and record changes. The operator coordinates this; founders respond to decisions rather than each preparing separate debate decks. Initial review: [two-round record](review.md).

Use the role folders for sanitized plans, task checklists and weekly summaries. Store identities, invoices, bank statements, cap tables, private customer content and unredacted investor notes in an approved private workspace, with access-controlled references in the summaries. Proposed filenames in the briefs are optional sections/views created when their triggering work exists; they are not a requirement to produce dozens of documents or evidence links that already exist.

**Keep week 1 to five shared records**, updated in place: (1) roster — COO; (2) dated cash sheet including the minimum operation — CFO; (3) offer card with existing brief/demo references — CMO, with COO/CEO inputs; (4) contact and counsel approval packet — COO assembles all inputs; (5) decision log — COO records the CEO/founder decisions. Customer responses and source evidence attach to these records. The initial brief/demo still has its two combined CEO/COO hour cap. Urgent counsel preparation remains in the approval packet, so reducing paperwork does not postpone it.

When actual work triggers expansion, **COO owns the single customer value/renewal record and investor next-action pipeline; CFO owns the single funnel/cost register and financial verification; CMO contributes campaign/source fields and permitted customer language; CEO contributes product evidence.** Role notes reference these records rather than recopying their numbers. A fuller deck is triggered by a useful meeting need; a data room by actual diligence; an onboarding checklist by an accepted account; expanded financial scenarios by the imminent decision they inform. Necessary dated cash facts are never deferred merely to keep a record short.

The four founder roles are the four workstreams. A task ID or a routine weekly update is not a new workstream completion. Run both Claude rounds at every major strategy checkpoint and actual workstream end as requested; combine coincident checkpoints into one complete packet covering all affected workstreams. Do not impose a cap that skips a later material decision.

Every task record needs: ID, owner, due date, current status, next action, dependency, expected result, evidence link and decision required. Status starts **planned**. Observed external outcomes must never be inferred from a document marked complete.

## Shared scorecard definitions

| Measure | Definition and owner |
|---|---|
| Qualified opportunity | Fits chosen group, has a named reputation task, a reachable buyer and an agreed next step; CMO/COO record raw count |
| Paying accounts and receipts | Actual customer payment cleared, with period and any refund/free access identified; CFO. Show current paying, cumulative first-time payers and churn separately |
| Activation | Intended account completes its first eligible real workflow correctly; capture setup effort separately. A demo or signup is not activation; COO/CEO |
| Owner value | Comparable baseline effort minus ongoing owner effort for work previously done. Include approvals, edits, uploads and supervision; show onboarding separately. Recalled estimates stay labeled; COO |
| Coverage and quality | Correctly completed eligible work / eligible work, with raw counts, missed work and corrections. No eligible events means insufficient activity, not automatic churn; CEO |
| Operator learning, if selected | Accepted instructions/rule version, repeated corrections, factual errors, unresolved work and intervention across comparable eligible tasks. Distinguish simulation, manual founder work, automated execution and verified external completion; COO/CEO use the existing customer record |
| Continued use and renewal | Continued delegation among accounts with eligible work; paid renewal among accounts actually due. Report numerator, denominator and cohort age; COO/CFO |
| Delivery cost | Vendor/payment costs plus disclosed founder/support effort, with actual cash costs and imputed labor separate; CFO |
| Acquisition economics | Spend and founder sales/marketing hours by channel, qualified opportunities, customers acquired and paid cohort value; CMO/CFO. No defensible long-term lifetime value from a short beta |
| Cash outlook | Cash received, dated obligations and forecast balance without uncommitted funding; CFO |
| Funding pipeline | Researched → approved contact → meeting → diligence → signed → cleared funds; COO owns next action, CFO verifies financial stages |
