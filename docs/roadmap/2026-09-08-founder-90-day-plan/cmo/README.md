# CMO: 90-day marketing and customer growth brief

Drafted September 7 for **September 8–December 6, 2026**, 90 calendar days inclusive. This is a work plan, not a report of completed marketing, customer acquisition or revenue.

Your job is to find a reachable type of business that will pay RepWorth to reduce its reputation workload, learn how to reach more of those owners, and help them keep receiving value after they buy. Marketing succeeds when suitable customers pay and continue using the service. A larger contact list, polished film or more followers is supporting activity.

The four founders are the CEO/CTO, COO, CFO and CMO. Each has stated availability of 40–60 hours a week; plan around **40**, with 60 an occasional agreed peak. The CFO helps marketing with prospect research, cost and funnel analysis. You own buyer selection proposals, messaging, channels, customer acquisition and marketing follow-through. The COO owns pitch preparation and pitches with the CEO; give them evidence and language rather than building a competing investor deck.

This role allocation replaces the older two-founder wording for this brief. The [shared founder plan](../README.md) owns assignments, common targets and acceleration gates. The [forward roadmap](../../2026-08-26-forward-roadmap.md) still owns product priorities, the [positioning](../../2026-08-26-positioning-governed-autonomy.md) owns claim limits, and the [verticals document](../../2026-08-26-verticals.md) owns candidate customer types.

## The marketing strategy to carry out

**September 9 offer addition:** work with CFO on one optional prepayment variant alongside the monthly offer, as specified in the shared plan. ### 1. Choose a buyer with real work and a reachable decision maker

Start with the actual beta cohort. Home services, beauty/personal care and food/beverage are candidates, not three simultaneous campaigns. Look for repeated owner effort, enough activity to observe during the month, access to the decision maker, usable existing inputs and willingness to discuss a real price. Prefer a cluster of similar owners over an attractive industry label. Keep low-volume and disinterested accounts in the evidence; do not select only flattering cases.

Fill this card by September 10 if evidence permits; otherwise identify the observation that will resolve it:

| Field | Working definition to validate |
|---|---|
| Customer type | One specific business type, location pattern and geography drawn from real participants. No selection has been made yet. |
| Buyer | The person responsible for Google reviews who can approve the purchase; verify whether that is the owner, manager or another role. |
| Recurring pain | Time spent drafting, checking, preparing lists and handling exceptions. Record the last actual occurrence and what the business currently does. |
| Reason to act now | A buyer's stated backlog, workload, staffing change or failed process. Never invent urgency from an unanswered public review. |
| Value proposition | “Handle Google review replies and requests with less owner effort, clear approval choices and a record you can check.” Quantified savings remain unproven until measured. |
| Offer | A guided walkthrough and agreed evaluation of the currently available Copilot/Autopilot workflow, followed by a concrete paid decision at the verified applicable price and start date. |
| Fit exclusions | Too little relevant work, no buying authority, no required access, or a must-have platform/workflow RepWorth cannot currently deliver. Preserve existing beta support promises. |

In discovery, ask about recent behavior, current workarounds, cost and unresolved frustration before giving the product pitch. YC's user-interview guidance supports grounding the discussion in the person's experience rather than asking whether they like an idea. [YC: How to Talk to Users](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/startup-school-week-1-recap-kevin-hale-and-eric-migicovsky/)

### 2. Start with direct founder conversations

First approach reachable prospects and beta users already on the roster. In parallel, prepare a small same-segment acquisition batch using relevant introductions if available, or one approved direct channel such as individually written business email. Begin that batch after the bounded first attempts; it need not wait for roster replies. Do not assume warm relationships or launch several campaigns at once.

CMO-04 owns the process: CFO researches publicly supported business fit and contact sources; you validate relevance and write messages; the CEO approves recipients or a bounded recipient group, message/template, channel and cadence. Reuse that approval within its scope. Track date, version, response and next action. Approval of copy alone does not approve an audience, spending or a bulk tool. Honor declines and requests to stop; silence is not permission.

For the next acquisition batch, prepare up to ten suitable businesses for review. Measure preparation and follow-up time before choosing a larger batch. Keep the same buyer, offer and channel while testing one change, such as the opening sentence or CTA. Small sequential batches provide directional learning, not a statistically proven winning message.

**First-contact draft, not authorized to send:**

> Hi [name], I’m [CMO], a co-founder of RepWorth. We help local businesses handle Google review replies and review requests. I noticed [specific, verified business fact]. I’d like to understand how you handle that work today and show you our current workflow. Would a 15-minute conversation be useful? If someone else owns this at [business], I’m happy to speak with them instead.

Replace every placeholder and remove the observation sentence if there is no useful verified fact. Do not imply affiliation with Google or claim that an unanswered review is costing this person sales. After a buyer has explained their problem, tailor the second message to that problem rather than inserting unsupported hours-saved numbers.

**Paid-decision draft:**

> Your agreed beta stays free until [date]. Based on the work we have reviewed, would you continue with [verified package and included scope] at [price, currency and billing period] from [date]? If not, what prevents that decision?

Ask for a specific answer and reason. Do not treat enthusiasm as commitment, shorten a free beta or offer future capabilities as included. CFO verifies the commercial terms; COO records the accepted obligations.

### 3. Use a page and demo that make the offer easy to assess

CMO-05 produces copy and an asset brief for an existing landing page: the named buyer's task, a short walkthrough, what is included, who approves what, the verified offer and one CTA. The CTA should book the guided walkthrough until self-serve setup and its commercial flow are verified. COO owns demos, scheduling and operational follow-through; CEO confirms the current route and booking method.

Give COO/CEO an outline for a 60–90-second real-product recording using existing screens: draft, owner approval or applicable automatic lane, completion and unresolved work. They own production; you check copy and labels for demo data, environment and mocked actions. Reuse COO's effort record covering setup, uploads and corrections; a short video is not a baseline. The elaborate film remains deferred. Public publication is a separate approval step.

Explain that voice examples activate after five posted, owner-edited replies. The Judge audits automatic replies after posting; its availability is checked before automatic posts, and unavailability routes the reply to owner review. The 15/30-minute veto window describes automatic replies, not every action. QR exists in code, but its availability needs a live check. Do not promote a planned connector, recovery flow or MCP interface as an available feature.

### 4. Learn what the buyer would choose instead

CMO-07 maintains a short comparison and objection record. Ask what the buyer uses or considered: NiceJob, TrueReview, GatherUp, Birdeye, Podium, an existing CRM, an agency, manual work or leaving the task undone. Record the actual objection, buyer type, alternative, our evidence-backed reply and whether the reply changed the decision. Public pricing needs a dated source and its conditions; do not quote an enterprise price as universal.

Birdeye documents review-response approval steps, so “only RepWorth offers human approval” is not a defensible claim. [Birdeye approval documentation](https://support.birdeye.com/en/articles/12829765-how-to-set-up-and-use-review-response-approvals)

GatherUp documents tickets, assignment and replies for first-party feedback in its Inbox article; this establishes a recovery-adjacent workflow, not every current platform capability. [GatherUp Inbox](https://gatherup.com/blog/inbox/)

Google itself documents review links and QR codes. A QR code alone is therefore not an exclusive reason to buy RepWorth; test the value of the complete owner workflow. [Google review links and QR codes](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en)

The four external pages above were checked September 7. They support those limited claims and interview methods, not our conversion assumptions or product availability. Use buyer evidence to explain why a particular owner should choose RepWorth, without claiming competitors cannot copy it.

### 5. Carry the customer through payment and continued value

| Suitable business reached | Correct fit, honest message and approved contact | CFO supplies research; record unique business and delivery/response facts. |

| Activated | Confirm a real eligible workflow completed and visible to the owner | CEO supplies product/backend evidence; signup alone is not activation. |
| Used again | Observe the next relevant task without silently doing it for the owner | COO coordinates support; separate owner minutes, founder assistance and product work. |
| First payment | Prepare the paid-decision message and help understand no/undecided answers | COO owns sales follow-up; CFO verifies cleared receipts; record future commitments separately. |
| Retained | At the agreed renewal point, check continued payment and useful recurring behavior | CFO reconciles billing; COO checks unresolved issues; report cohorts only after their observation period has elapsed. |
| Referral | Ask for an introduction after the owner has received value and consented to discuss it | A suggested name is not an introduction, and an introduction is not a sale. |

CMO-08 uses COO's common owner-effort record to prepare permitted buyer proof. Join selected observations when useful; do not run a duplicate diary or interview program. Preserve negative results and include approvals, uploads, corrections, supervision and separately stated onboarding. Work formerly ignored is added coverage, not historical time saved. CFO supplies support/provider costs. Bookings need business records, with association separated from causation. Low review volume can make the value claim inconclusive.

CMO-09 owns messaging and permitted proof for continuation and referrals; COO owns each customer's renewal, support and next action. Agree with COO on a check-in after the first completed task and another before the beta or billing decision. For an inactive account, help identify whether the problem is access, no work to do, poor output, too much effort or lack of value. Do not send generic reactivation messages that ignore an unresolved support problem. Marketing promises must not exceed the support capacity COO has confirmed.

### 6. Earn a referral or partner route before expanding it

Keep customer referral requests as part of the existing journey, not a second broad campaign. A simple approved draft is: “If this has been useful, is there another [selected business type] owner you would feel comfortable introducing us to?” No reward or paid affiliate commitment is implied.

CMO-10 prepares a partner option only after learning the direct channel: a local website/marketing provider, consultant or trade network serving the same buyer, chosen from actual access. Verify who they serve, whether they can introduce decision makers, their incentive and the support they expect. Do not promise white-label, API access, exclusivity or commissions. If founders choose the partner experiment, pause the previous acquisition experiment; test one approved introduction and measure its full cost and outcome before announcing a program. Research can stay parked without becoming a live second campaign.

## Measurement, funnel math and decisions

CMO-11 contributes campaign fields and recommends channel decisions; **CFO owns the canonical funnel and cost register**. Add timestamps, campaign version and original source to its shared business IDs. Preserve assisting sources so a referral and email do not become two acquired accounts. COO owns customer-value and renewal records. Use their views/references instead of parallel CMO truth files. Private details remain in an approved restricted system; repository evidence uses sanitized references.

For each dated batch, report unique suitable businesses researched, approved, successfully reached, replied, qualified conversations held, evaluations started, activated, first payments, amounts received and elapsed days. Then report repeat usage and due renewals. Every percentage states its denominator and window. Missing data is unknown; a zero denominator is not evaluable. Exclude duplicates but disclose exclusions and invalid contact attempts. Do not mix mature and still-waiting batches to manufacture improvement.

Show cash acquisition cost separately from an economic-cost scenario that includes founder sales/marketing hours at an explicit assumed rate. Divide by verified new paying customers from the cohort; zero payers means acquisition cost per payer is not yet calculable. CFO also models delivery cost, contribution after service costs and cash payback. Do not invent lifetime value from a few weeks of beta use.

**Initial learning allowance:** propose a total contact cap, dates, hours and supported customer intake before starting. The first ten researched businesses are a quality-check subset, not necessarily the entire month's sample. An illustrative ceiling is 60 reached businesses per week within the time model below; actual approval may be lower. Three payers are not required to conduct this explicitly approved initial discovery test. The shared acceleration gate applies to expansion beyond that allowance. Do not wait for every prospect's response before preparing the next permitted subset, and do not treat a researched list as permission to contact it.

**Illustrative capacity calculation only; none of these rates is measured:**

| Input | Hypothetical value |
|---|---:|
| Unique businesses reached per full acquisition week | 60 |
| Reached → qualified conversation held | 10% |
| Qualified conversation → activation | 60% |
| Activation → first payment | 40% |
| First payments per batch after sufficient time | 60 × 10% × 60% × 40% = 1.44 expected |
| Ten such batches | 600 reached; 14.4 expected first payments before adjusting for lag |
| Contacts required for 25 first payments at those rates | 25 ÷ (10% × 60% × 40%) = about 1,042 reached, or 105/week over ten weeks |
| Contacts required for 100 first payments at those rates | About 4,167, or 417/week over ten weeks |

At an assumed eight minutes per business for personalization and follow-up, 60/week uses eight hours. The illustrative 25-first-payment case needs about 14 hours/week; the 100 case needs about 56, before conversations and other duties. These are **first payments, not retained current paying accounts**. Better conversion or extra funded capacity cannot be assumed. A hypothetical renewal rate applies only to accounts actually due. Free-beta terms and sales-cycle lag can move receipts beyond the cash deadline or December 6; these examples do not set milestone targets.

Use the following **provisional decision rules**, recorded before evaluating the batch. They are small-sample triage rules, not statistical proof:

- After 30 valid businesses are reached and each has had the approved response/follow-up window, zero qualified conversations triggers a pause in new intake. Check contact validity, buyer fit, message and channel; change one factor before another batch. Propose a ten-business-day observation window, shortened only when the cash deadline demands an earlier inconclusive decision.
- After five qualified conversations without activation, inspect the offer and onboarding barrier with COO/CEO. After five usable activations and concrete paid offers without a paid yes, inspect actual value, price and terms. Do not solve either by buying more traffic. Fewer observations means weaker evidence, not permission to claim success.
- Pause expansion immediately when the offer is inaccurate, a serious unresolved product issue undermines delivery, support exceeds agreed capacity or the CFO cannot establish affordable continuation. Preserve existing customer obligations; founders decide changes.
- Apply the [shared acceleration gate](../README.md#when-to-accelerate) before increasing intake or spend, using its duration, volume and cash/support limits. Do not maintain a second set of thresholds here. CFO may present explicitly bounded early losses; positive company-wide profit is not required. Small batches remain directional evidence, not product-market fit.
- If referral or partner access looks stronger, write a replacement experiment with the same cost and evidence requirements. Do not quietly run both primary channels. Do not use AI-search visibility or social reach as a substitute for qualified conversations, payments and continued value.
