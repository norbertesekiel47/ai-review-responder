Read the [shared four-founder plan](../README.md) first. It owns company
targets, scorecard definitions, approval scope and the weekly rhythm; this
brief assigns the COO's part.

This is a proposed operating assignment for the four-founder team, not a grant
of legal authority. It supersedes older two-founder ownership assumptions for
this plan. The CEO is also CTO; the COO owns pitch preparation and pitches
alongside the CEO; the CFO owns finance and also helps the CMO; the CMO owns
audience, market and presentation of proof from COO's customer records. Each founder reports
40–60 available hours per week. Plan against **40**, with buffer; 60 is optional
peak availability, not the recurring workload or a delivery promise.

**Status: drafted, not executed.** No customer or investor was contacted, no
meeting booked, no legal review commissioned and no money spent through this
brief. The detailed register is a reference menu, not a requirement to create
ten documents. Only the five shared records below are required in week 1;
later filenames describe optional sections, views or exports triggered by real
customer or investor work, not evidence that results already exist.

## Your mission and the facts you start with

Make it easy for a suitable customer or investor to reach a concrete decision
about RepWorth, and make sure the company delivers what it has promised.
Own the process from qualified introduction through meeting, next step and
customer handoff. Give the CEO a concise decision queue rather than making
the CEO maintain the pipeline or assemble every pitch.

The last report was **zero paying customers**, approximately five interested
prospects, and an unspecified beta cohort. Those are different groups. The
reported runway is one to two months; actual cash, obligations and the binding
date remain unverified. Do not claim an investor pipeline, customer volume,
retention or revenue that has not been recorded.

The customer outcome to test is less recurring owner work, with booking or
revenue evidence only where supportable. Select one customer type from the
actual beta cohort. Existing Copilot/Autopilot, Google replies, review requests
and QR provide material for an honest demonstration. A new feature is not a
prerequisite for a sales or investor conversation.

Use the [forward roadmap](../../2026-08-26-forward-roadmap.md),

## The investor brief and ten-slide deck

The short brief should answer who buys, what RepWorth handles, what is
implemented, what is unproven, why the team is investigating this opportunity,
and what conversation is being requested. Use CFO's verified ask when ready;
otherwise state that the amount is being reconciled. Do not invent a raise
size to make the page look finished.

After initial conversations identify useful missing evidence, expand the
single deck. Prefer direct screenshots, simple numbers and sources over
animation. There is no independent deadline to produce a ten-slide deck;
create it when a real meeting or investor request needs it. Label missing
evidence rather than delaying all discussions.

| Slide | What it should communicate | Required input/evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem | A specific owner's recurring reputation workload and why it matters | CMO discovery plus COO observations, task volumes and minutes; no invented booking losses |
| 2. Buyer | One provisional customer type, decision-maker, current tools and buying trigger | Actual cohort/qualified conversations; unknowns visible |
| 3. Product demo | One complete existing journey, including owner decisions and exceptions | CEO-verified product scope; dated demo with fixture/staging/production labels |
| 4. Customer proof | Usage, paid decisions, effort returned and what the beta still cannot prove | Last reported zero paid stays until evidence changes; distinguish interested prospects, active beta, paid commitments and received cash |
| 5. Market | A bottom-up view of the reachable first segment and plausible expansion | CMO's sourced business counts/eligibility and buyer budgets, CFO's pricing scenarios; assumptions and method visible |
| 6. Alternatives and why win | What buyers use today and the specific reason to choose RepWorth | Competitors, CRM/agency/manual alternatives; observed paid preference rather than “AI-native is our moat” |
| 7. Distribution | How the first suitable buyers can be reached and served repeatedly | CMO's channel context plus CFO's canonical funnel: lead → demo → paid stages with actual denominators and elapsed time |
| 8. Economics | Price, collection timing, delivery/support costs and remaining uncertainty | CFO's verified model, actual usage/receipts where available; historical service allowance is not full burn |
| 9. Team | CEO/CTO, COO, CFO and CMO ownership, relevant experience and demonstrated delivery | Accurate biographies, committed capacity and actual achievements; no invented credentials |
| 10. Ask and milestones | Amount, intended use, cash timing and evidence the funded period should produce | CFO's verified funding/cash case, CEO's decision and conditional milestones; no guarantee of closing or scaling |

Before each pitch, COO prepares a one-page meeting brief: investor fit, the
meeting's purpose, participants, available time, three questions and the
specific next step sought. COO and CEO rehearse the opening and product
handoff once, initially capped at 30 minutes. COO owns flow and follow-through;
CEO explains product, technical judgment and company direction. CFO joins
finance-heavy diligence when useful. Keep a shared Q&A on buyer value,
competitors, repeat use, distribution, economics, team and ask. “We don't yet
know; here is the test and owner” is an acceptable answer.

After each approved conversation, record what was asked, what evidence was
requested and the date/owner of the next step. Draft a concise follow-up with
the agreed recap, useful material and one concrete request. Queue the exact
message and recipient for CEO approval unless a recorded standing scope
already covers them; this plan itself does not authorize sending.
Repeated objections trigger investigation under the roadmap's decision rules,
not automatic investor-driven feature promises.

## Pipeline and data room

The COO maintains one private investor pipeline as a working view triggered
by actual investor preparation/follow-through; it is not a sixth mandatory
week-1 document. Minimum fields: contact and
firm; source/introduction; stage/sector/geography fit; relationship owner;
approval status; stage; last actual interaction; investor decision process;
requested evidence; next action/date; amount discussed; conditions; earliest
credible clearing date; supporting source; and outcome. Mark researched,
approved-to-contact, contacted, met, diligence, proposed terms, signed,
received, passed or parked separately. Do not assign funding probabilities
without a basis or count names as interested investors.

Useful process targets, explicitly assumptions: record agreed next steps
within one working day; give every active row an owner/date; flag missed
follow-ups after one working day. Start with up to five well-matched investor
candidates after geography, stage and access are known; expand from actual
response and meeting lag. A large list is not the success metric.

Create a data-room index when actual diligence requires it, not as preparation
for seeking the first conversation. It covers: entity/ownership records and required approvals;
founder/IP agreements; CFO financial model, actual cash and use of funds;
product demonstration and current scope; customer/beta agreements and
permissioned proof; market/distribution evidence; existing legal/privacy
materials; material vendor/service obligations; and an investor Q&A.
CFO supplies finance/entity references; CMO supplies market/customer sources;
CEO supplies architecture/product evidence. COO checks completeness and
access. Do not copy sensitive originals into this repository or share the
room externally without CEO approval and appropriate access decisions.

**Signed is not received.** COO tracks commercial/legal progression; CFO
confirms when unrestricted funds actually clear and their obligations.
Proposed terms, signed documents and future subscription start dates remain
separate from available cash. Never show the ask as achieved from pipeline
stage alone.

## Customer handoff, delivery and renewal

CMO hands off a qualified business with its consent/contact status, problem,
decision-maker, current software, prior notes and expectations. COO accepts
or returns a specific missing item; avoid restarting discovery. Use one demo
to answer the buyer's real concern and reach yes/no/defer. Record the actual
offer, scope, start date and existing beta/founding promises. A refusal is
useful evidence; it does not authorize changing price or adding features.

For an accepted account, coordinate: agreed scope → account/location setup →
correct review connection → owner settings/approval choice → first completed
task → owner sees the result and next action. Obtain CEO/backend confirmation
for product outcomes when needed. Failed setup or founder rescue is recorded,
not marked successful because an invitation was sent. Avoid collecting
passwords in notes. Do not promise every review will be answered or that QR
scans create sales.

Support starts with one queue and an owner, not a new help-center project.
Record account alias, impact, first-seen time, reproducible steps, current
workaround, owner, next-update time, resolution and evidence. As a proposed
internal target, triage during the working day and acknowledge by the next
working day; confirm coverage before promising any customer SLA. Escalate
suspected unintended public actions, wrong-account behavior or unavailable
core service to CEO immediately. Any customer update is drafted for approval
unless a specific standing communication authorization already covers it.

Send a product request to CEO only with the named account, paid-decision
blocker or observed quality failure affecting an existing beta promise,
evidence, workaround and expected benefit. CEO can reject vague UI polish.
The first new feature has a provisional **20-hour total engineering cap**,
including discovery and verification, with at most four hours of connector
investigation inside it. COO does not allocate that cap independently or
promise it to buyers. No qualifying problem means no new feature.

Before each actual beta end or renewal date, prepare the continuation
conversation from existing notes and support history. Ask what work was
handled, what still required effort, whether the price is worth paying, and
why the owner continues, defers or leaves. Keep voluntary churn, payment
failure and incomplete onboarding distinct. Report individual results and
denominators, not a churn rate from a tiny unfinished cohort. Feed the CMO
permissioned customer proof and the CFO verified commercial decisions.

## Weekly cadence, checkpoints and stop rules

The contact/counsel packet has independent decisions: (1) roster contact under verified existing terms, ready for CEO decision September 8 evening; (2) counsel engagement or spend; (3) the optional prepayment offer CFO/CMO prepare September 9. Missing counsel information does not automatically delay eligible contact. Existing communication permission is checked, never inferred from being on a prospect list. A new offer needs its own approved amount, terms and message before use.

Offer the approved monthly terms and, only if approved, the shared plan's voluntary prepayment option. Preserve free beta dates and promised support. Record a paid yes, funds received and current payer state separately; CFO records future service obligations from prepayment. Obtain each founder's own availability agreement through CFO before scheduling that person under a reduced-support scenario. Reduction/pause is itself a material strategy checkpoint, with both Claude rounds.

Follow the shared rhythm: **Monday 30-minute founder decision meeting;
Wednesday brief written numbers/blockers; Friday 30-minute results review**.
Founders provide five-line reports; COO keeps one decision log and updates the
shared plan rather than rewriting every report. Keep report/agenda preparation
to about one hour weekly, alongside those meetings within the allocation above.

Approval may cover one action or a bounded standing scope: recipients/segment,
channel, purpose, message/template, cadence and any budget. Record CEO approval
once and reuse it within those bounds; routine follow-ups do not need another
decision unless scope changes. No such authorization is inferred from this
draft. Batch pending requests with the exact scope, reason, deadline and
artifact. Silence is not approval; continue independent preparation while blocked.

Use this format for the COO's weekly report:

```text
done: Completed work or result, distinguished from drafts.
blocked: Missing input/approval, owner, date and consequence.
next: The next concrete action and its owner/date.
evidence: Openable sanitized artifacts or authorized private references.
risk: Cash, delivery, capacity or claim risk and proposed response.
```

At day 30 **October 7**, day 60 **November 6**, day 90 **December 6**, each
workstream end and any material strategy commitment, COO prepares the full
current plan/evidence packet for the coordinator's **two Claude challenge
rounds**. Task completion alone is not a workstream end. When role ends and
major checkpoints coincide, use one full four-role packet and one two-round
review covering them; no mandatory checkpoint is skipped. Round 1 takes
three strongest objections seriously and revises;
round 2 reviews the revision. Save both inputs/outputs and a short objection
→ change table. Do not replace the required debate with another routine
meeting or silently substitute a reviewer. Reserve checkpoint preparation
inside planned hours and reduce optional collateral if necessary.

Cash review is earlier: CFO sheet due September 8, approved roster offer
attempts by September 10, decision review September 14, and cash gate
September 21 or the earlier actual need. COO surfaces missing inputs the
same day. CFO tests funded continuation and the minimum no-new-money floor,
including dated customer-support/data obligations and founder support, at
that earliest cash decision. If neither is covered, prepare the smaller-scope
or pause decision then, not at day 30. CEO decides. If no decision
is reached at the operative gate, new discretionary feature/spending
commitments stay on hold while existing authorized obligations continue.
COO proposes the operational consequences; no automatic cancellations,
customer messages or shutdown actions follow from a missed target.

A workstream ends only with inspectable acceptance evidence and its checkpoint
decision. A deck is not investment; a meeting is not a commitment; a signed
commitment is not available cash; an onboarding invitation is not delivered
value. Keep those distinctions in every report.
