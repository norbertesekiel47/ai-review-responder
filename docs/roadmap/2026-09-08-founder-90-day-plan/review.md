# Two-round Claude challenge — founder plan

Recorded September 7, 2026. Scope: the [shared plan](README.md) and all four role briefs, drafted for September 8–December 6. This is review evidence for a proposed plan, not proof of customer, financial or operational results.

## Method and outcome

Both rounds used the installed Claude CLI through the existing authenticated Max subscription. The reviewer received the confirmed bottleneck, the four-founder correction, 40–60 available hours, CFO marketing support and the complete five-document plan. Round 2 also received the first response and a description of changes. Tools, external MCP servers, local customizations and persistent reviewer sessions were disabled. No API key was used. Both calls returned successful single-turn results. CLI model-use metadata lists `claude-fable-5-1` and `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`; this record does not relabel the reviewer as a different model.

Round 1 requested revision. Round 2 said **approve with three conditions**. We applied those compatible conditions and checked the final documents locally. The final amendments were not sent for a third round; this is not a claim of unconditional approval of every final sentence.

<summary>Full Claude response — round 1</summary>

**Round 1 verdict: revise before adoption.** The plan is honest about what it does not know, and that honesty is its main strength. Its weakness is that it is a process architecture sized for a funded ten-person company, laid over four founders with zero paying customers and an estimated one to two months of cash. Below are the three objections that matter most, each with a minimal amendment.

## Objection 1: the calendar assumes cash the plan admits may not exist

Facts: runway was last estimated at one to two months. Zero paying customers. Cash-needed date unknown.

Prediction, not fact: on that estimate, cash-out plausibly lands between Day 30 and Day 60. The Day-60 review on November 6 is where acceleration would be approved. The plan requires investor money for acceleration, requires customer evidence for investor money, and expects that evidence to arrive in weeks 3 to 8. Even a fast seed process from first meeting to cleared funds usually takes longer than the window that leaves. So weeks 9 to 13 describe a company that only exists if something unstated happens. Twenty-five paying accounts would not close the gap either. The CFO brief already concedes that dozens of low-price subscriptions are far below the funding need, yet the account targets still sit in the milestone table as if they were the goal.

**Amendment.** On September 8, the CFO defines a "no-new-money floor": the smallest set of obligations that keeps existing beta commitments honored, plus each founder's minimum monthly need. Day 30 becomes a binary decision between floor and funded, recorded in the decision log. Replace the week 9 to 13 tables in all four briefs with one paragraph of conditional options, activated only if cleared cash covers them. Nothing else in the plan changes.

**Why it matters.** A detailed November schedule creates false confidence and pulls founder hours into preparing for a period that may not arrive.

## Objection 2: the marketing model cannot produce the targets it is asked to support

Facts: roughly five interested prospects exist. The CMO's own illustrative funnel gives 1.44 first payments per 60-contact batch. Those rates are unmeasured.

Arithmetic on the plan's own numbers: reaching 25 current payers by December 6 at that yield needs roughly 17 full batches. Weeks 1 and 2 are setup and cash gate. Free beta terms and sales lag delay payment further. The plan says to "confront this gap on September 14," but the 3/10/25 figures are copied into four files, so recalibration means editing four documents that will drift. The only plausible route to three payers by October 7 is converting the existing five prospects and beta users. That is a different task from cold outreach and it is buried under the outreach experiment.

**Amendment.** Keep the numbers in the shared README only; the role briefs link to it. Rewrite the Day-30 target as "three paid yes decisions from the existing roster, cash cleared where due." Set Day-60 and Day-90 targets on October 7 from measured batch rates, not now. Make the first two weeks of CMO and COO effort the roster conversion, with cold outreach starting only after every reachable existing prospect has been asked.

**Why it matters.** Unreachable targets get quietly reinterpreted. The plan forbids fabricated traction, but the pressure a wrong number creates is real.

## Objection 3: founders will not do this much process, so they will fake completion or skip the customers

Fact: the four briefs propose over 40 named deliverables, most due within 48 hours. The COO alone owes a roster, brief, demo, counsel comparison, agenda, decision log, onboarding checklist and support route in two days. The CFO owes a three-scenario 13-week forecast by September 9.

Duplicated ownership is the concrete symptom. Roster reconciliation appears in CEO-01, COO-01 and CMO-01. Customer value is measured under CEO-07, COO's owner-value scorecard, CMO-08 and CFO-08. Renewal reasons sit in COO-09, CMO-09 and CFO-04. Funnel counts live in CMO-11 and CFO-07. Each pair says the other "reuses" the record, but each also names its own file. That is four spreadsheets with conflicting totals, which the README itself warns against.

There is also an internal contradiction: the CEO brief caps the first improvement at 20 total engineering hours yet allocates 16 product hours every week for 13 weeks. And "two Claude challenge rounds at each workstream completion" could mean dozens of review cycles if every task ID counts as a workstream.

**Amendment.** Cut week 1 to five artifacts: roster, cash sheet, offer card, first contact approval packet, decision log. Every other file is created when its trigger event occurs. Assign one record per concept: COO owns the customer value and renewal record, CFO owns the funnel and cost sheet, CMO and CEO contribute lines but keep no parallel file. State that challenge rounds run at the three dated checkpoints plus at most one material strategy change per period, preserving the founder's requirement without exploding its count. Reconcile the CEO's weekly product hours to the 20-hour cap.

**Why it matters.** The permission-before-contact rule, five-line reports and two challenge rounds are the constraints worth protecting. Burying them under 40 templates is how they get abandoned.

## On blitzscaling

The plan correctly refuses to call itself blitzscaling, and that is right. Hoffman and Yeh's own framework puts a four-person company with no paying customers in the "do things that don't scale" stage. Real blitzscaling burns cash for market position. There is no cash to burn. The one honest application of speed-over-efficiency here is spending hours 41 to 60 on conversations with the five prospects this week, not on documentation. Say that plainly instead of dressing a 2× two-week expansion rule in acceleration language.

**Conditions for round 2:** the three amendments applied, targets consolidated, and the September 8 floor definition included in the revision.

<summary>Full Claude response — round 2</summary>

**Round 2 verdict: approve with three conditions.** The round 1 conditions were met: the floor is defined on September 8, targets live in one place, week 1 is five records, and record ownership is single. The retained 13-week tables are the founder's call and are now clearly conditional. What remains are three gaps that the revision exposed rather than created.

## Objection 1: the plan has no customer-side lever that produces cash before the cash gate

Facts: the bottleneck is cash. The Day-30 aspiration is three paid yes decisions. A yes with a future start "is not money received." Beta terms are honored. CFO-08 states no tariff change is proposed, and the CMO offer card forbids any "invented discount." So the offer is a monthly subscription at the current price, starting after beta ends.

Consequence: the plan can hit its own Day-30 target while contributing zero to the September 21 or October 7 cash picture. The only sources that can move cash on those dates are cost cuts, founder money, or customer prepayment. The plan models the first, is silent on the second (see objection 2), and structurally excludes the third.

**Amendment.** On September 9, CFO and CMO add one cash-forward variant to the offer card: a prepaid quarter or year at a stated discount, or a paid onboarding fee for founder-delivered setup. CEO approves it alongside the monthly price. Beta users may voluntarily choose an earlier paid start; nobody's free period is shortened. The Day-30 line reports yes decisions, cash received, and current payers as three numbers. Whether anyone accepts is unknown; the point is that the plan currently cannot find out.

**Why it matters.** This is the one action inside the founders' control that can change the September 21 decision. Leaving it out makes the gate a pure cost-cutting exercise.

## Objection 2: founder availability is aggregated, but the decision it implies is individual

Facts: the floor includes "minimum founder support." The plan says an unpaid founder role "cannot be assumed available indefinitely" and warns against assuming unpaid labor. The CEO records the September 21 decision. Founder compensation needs are listed as missing.

The gap: if the floor cannot cover support, the real decision is whether each of four people continues at reduced or zero pay, and for how long. That is not the CEO's to make. The plan never asks each founder for their own binding date and their own answer. Every 40-hour allocation in all four briefs rests on an assumption that has not been checked with the people it concerns.

**Amendment.** On September 8, each founder gives CFO privately: personal binding date, minimum monthly need, and whether they continue at floor-level support and until when. The September 21 packet shows the funded, floor, and reduce options with named founder availability under each. If the outcome is reduction or pause, it counts as a material strategy checkpoint and gets the two Claude rounds the user requires. This is not a new document; it is four private lines feeding an existing record.

**Why it matters.** A floor that assumes four people who have not agreed to it is not a floor. This is also the input the visa-related timeline needs, handled by counsel, not by this plan.

## Objection 3: week 1 couples the fastest action to the slowest dependency

Facts: the contact and counsel approval packet is one record. Counsel screening "starts now"; contact "waits for approval." The COO owes the roster, offer card, packet, and decision log by September 9. CFO needs inputs from all four founders the same day. The COO's steady-state week allocates 16 hours to investor process and deck with no investor list yet identified.

Risk, a prediction rather than an observed failure: a CEO reading one packet containing legal questions and outreach scope will tend to wait for the legal section before approving contact. The September 10 roster deadline then slips for a reason unrelated to the roster. Separately, five prospects who have already given contact permission are the highest-yield work in the company, and the COO's brief devotes more hours to investors who have not been named.

**Amendment.** Keep one packet, but with two separately dated CEO approval lines: roster contact, decidable September 8 evening for contacts with existing permission using current beta terms; and counsel engagement or spend. Neither waits on the other. In weeks 1 and 2, COO shifts eight investor hours to roster conversion; the brief already allows legal and cash work to displace deck polish, so extend that to customer work. Investor hours return when a named candidate exists.

**Why it matters.** The plan's own priority is the existing roster by September 10. The packet design and hour allocation quietly work against it.

## On blitzscaling

The revised framing is responsible. Speed over efficiency is applied where it is real: four founders in parallel, selling the current workflow, manual onboarding shown as delivery cost. Acceleration is gated on measured batches and cleared cash. That is Hoffman and Yeh's "do things that don't scale" stage, named honestly, not cautious planning renamed. The 2× two-week expansion rule is fine as a starting rule. The one missing piece is objection 1: real blitzscaling teams pull cash forward aggressively, and this plan forbids the pricing moves that do it.

## What is fact and what is not

Facts used above: zero paid, roughly five prospects, unknown beta and cash dates, four founders at 40 to 60 hours, the plan text itself. Predictions: that a monthly offer with deferred start produces no cash by September 21, that packet bundling delays contact approval, and that unnamed investors will not clear money in this window. None is observed yet. The amendments are cheap enough that being wrong costs little.

**Conditions for adoption:** cash-forward offer variant on the September 9 card; per-founder availability lines into the September 8 cash sheet; split approval lines in the packet with the COO hour shift. All approval, five-line report, and two-round debate requirements stay as written.
