> **The send governors (#898) — why they are two modules and not one.**
> Four things stand between an approved batch and a physical send, in a fixed
> order: **breaker → platform ceiling → account cap → per-child** (#622 §4.3).
> `send-breaker.ts` owns the first; `send-caps.ts` owns the middle two; the
> per-child checks belong to the **Dispatch gate**. Amendment **B14** adds a
> fourth release-level check beside the caps — `subscriptionGrantsFullAccess`,
> moved out of the Dispatch gate because an account-level fact every child would
> answer identically could only ever *defer*, and #900 CASes the outreach row to
> `queued` before the child runs, so a deferring child strands it. They are split because they
> answer different questions with different costs and different failure
> directions — the breaker is a *reputation* verdict that must fail CLOSED even
> when the database is unavailable, and the caps are *volume* arithmetic whose
> only fail-closed obligation is a missing env var resolving to 0. Collapsing
> them would give one module two failure contracts, and would put the expensive
> 7-day aggregate on the send loop.
> **#898 ships all four as callable modules and wires NO order** — nothing here
> sends, and nothing here is read by the send path yet; **#900 is the consumer**
> that applies the order in the release decision, and its releaser is a *pure
> reader* of the breaker so the trip stays the `HealthAlertPoller`'s job alone.
> The breaker is a **platform singleton, not a per-tenant dial**, because the
> asset it protects is the shared **Sending identity** — one mailbox, one sending
> domain, every account. It never self-clears; the operator procedure (diagnosis
> order, the `/admin/solicitation` reset, and why a reset is a real recovery —
> it moves the window's lower edge to the reset instant, so only post-reset
> activity is measured and `BREAKER_MIN_SENDS` turns that into a bounded ~100-send
> half-open probe rather than a re-trip) is
> `docs/runbooks/solicitation-sending-domain.md` §8, and the ramp it is the
> automatic counterpart to is that runbook's §5.
